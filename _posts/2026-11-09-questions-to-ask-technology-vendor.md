The sales call runs forty minutes. The demo looks polished, the rep answers every question with confidence, and the order form arrives that afternoon with a discount that expires Friday. You sign a three-year agreement based on a conversation you can't enforce.

Print this script and bring it to the next vendor call. Read the questions out loud, in order, and write the answers down while the rep talks.

## Protecting your money

> "Walk me through what we pay in year three, including every increase the contract allows."

First-year pricing is a sales tool. The renewal clause sets your real cost.

> "Show me which features from the demo cost extra."

Demos run on the top plan. Ask the rep to point at each screen and name the tier it belongs to.

> "Define a user for billing purposes, and tell me what happens to the invoice when we hire two people."

Some products bill per seat, some per location, and some for anyone who logs in once a month.

> "Tell me what it costs to add the features we'll want in year two."

You'll outgrow the starter plan. Price the upgrade now, while the rep wants the deal, instead of in eighteen months when switching feels impossible.

## Protecting your data

> "Tell me where you store our data, and in which country."

> "Do you use our data to train AI models, and where does the contract say so?"

Ask for the clause number. A verbal "no" on this question means nothing until you see it in writing.

> "If you have a breach, how fast do you notify us, and in what form?"

> "Send us your most recent independent security audit, such as a SOC 2 report."

A vendor that stores client data should have an outside firm checking its controls. If the rep offers a marketing page about security instead of a report, note it and ask again in writing.

## Protecting your way out

> "Show me how we export everything we've put into your system, and in what file format."

Ask the rep to demonstrate the export during the call. If the export produces a format no other product can read, you're signing up for a second migration project on the day you leave.

> "Tell me the notice period on the renewal clause, and the date it falls on."

Put that date in your calendar the day you sign, with a reminder sixty days earlier.

> "After we cancel, how long do you keep our data, and how do we confirm you deleted it?"

## Protecting your Friday afternoons

> "Give me the support hours, the response time in the contract, and the name of the person we call when it breaks."

> "Give me two customers our size I can call this week."

A vendor with happy customers your size produces two names before the call ends. Hesitation tells you the product fits bigger or smaller firms.

## Reading the answers

Pay attention to how the rep answers, as well as what they say. A rep who pulls up the contract and reads you the clause has handled these questions before. A rep who says "I'd have to check with legal" on the data questions has given you your next step: ask for the answer in writing before you sign.

Watch for three phrases in particular: "that's on the roadmap," "we can probably work something out," and "most customers don't ask about that." Each one describes something the contract won't guarantee.

## One rule for the contract

Move every promise from the call into the order form or the contract before you sign. Sales reps change jobs, and you can't enforce what a former rep told you on a video call in November.

If the vendor refuses to write a promise down, you've learned what the promise is worth.
