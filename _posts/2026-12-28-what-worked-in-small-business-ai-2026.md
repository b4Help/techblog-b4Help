Two respected surveys measured small-business AI use, and their numbers sit far apart. One puts small-business use of generative AI at 58 percent. The other puts U.S. business use of AI at about one in five. Both numbers come from real surveys, and the distance between them tells you more about 2026 than either figure alone.

A caution before the numbers: none of these surveys measured whether AI paid off. They measured who used it and for what. I've kept this review to what the evidence supports.

## Two surveys, two different questions

The U.S. Chamber of Commerce's fourth *Empowering Small Business* report, released in August 2025, found that 58 percent of small businesses say they use generative AI, up from 40 percent in 2024 and more than double the 2023 rate ([U.S. Chamber of Commerce](https://www.uschamber.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/u-s-chambers-latest-empowering-small-business-report-shows-majority-of-businesses-in-all-50-states-are-embracing-ai)). The Chamber also advocates for a single national AI framework, which it states in the same release, so read it as a survey from an organization with a policy position.

The Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey asks firms whether they used AI in any business function over the past two weeks. Across December 2025 to May 2026, that rate hovered between 17 and 20 percent, and sat at 19.8 percent as of May 3, 2026 ([U.S. Census Bureau, May 2026](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html)). The Bureau revised its wording in November 2025, from AI use "in producing goods or services" to use "in any business function."

"Do you use generative AI?" and "did your business use AI in a business function in the last two weeks?" measure different things. An owner who drafts an occasional email with ChatGPT answers yes to the first and may answer no to the second. Keep both numbers in mind whenever a vendor quotes you an adoption statistic.

## Staff used AI to draft and research

The clearest evidence on how people used AI came from workers themselves. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce Foundation and Ipsos surveyed 1,070 employees of businesses with 2 to 499 staff in May 2026 for their first Main Street AI Monitor ([U.S. Chamber Foundation, June 2026](https://www.uschamberfoundation.org/workforce/half-of-small-business-workers-use-ai-most-to-boost-productivity-not-automate-jobs)).

Among workers who use AI, 64 percent said their main use is personal productivity: drafting, summarizing and brainstorming. Among those whose jobs include each task, 90 percent use AI for writing and editing communications and 88 percent for research and information gathering. Six percent use AI to automate workflows with minimal human involvement.

The Foundation calls the dominant pattern augmentation: workers using AI to do their jobs better. Full automation remained rare.

## Employees moved before owners set rules

The same survey found that 19 percent of workers said employees exploring tools on their own drove most of the AI adoption at their organization, against 11 percent who said it came from organizational guidance. About one in ten said their employer offered formal AI training. Forty-seven percent named privacy or security concerns as a barrier.

Put those together and you get the gap that worries me most for small professional firms. Staff adopted the tools first, few received training, and privacy led the barriers they reported. If your firm holds client files, that combination means someone may have pasted confidential material into a public tool without anyone deciding whether that was allowed.

## Size still shaped who adopted

Firm size mattered in both the government and Chamber data. Census found AI use rose among firms with at least 20 employees between December 2025 and May 2026, but showed no significant change among firms with fewer than 20, and under 20 percent of firms with four or fewer employees reported using AI. In the Chamber Foundation survey, 43 percent of businesses with two to nine employees used AI for work tasks, against 59 percent of businesses with 100 to 249.

Sector mattered too. Census put AI use at 39.7 percent in the Information sector and 33.9 percent in Finance and Insurance as of May 2026, against around 14 percent in Retail Trade.

## Carrying it into 2027

For a small firm, the 2026 evidence points to two things worth acting on.

**Build on the use that works.** Staff used AI to help with their own work, and few handed whole workflows to software. Grow that before you pay for autonomy.

**Write the policy your staff are waiting for.** Adoption came from employees, training stayed rare, and privacy led the barriers they named. A one-page rule on approved tools and off-limits data closes the most dangerous gap for little cost.

The size data adds one more point: the smallest firms adopted least, which leaves them the most room to catch up.
