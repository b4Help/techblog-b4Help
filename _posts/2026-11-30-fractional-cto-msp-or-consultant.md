You call three companies about the same technology headache. The first quotes a monthly price per user. The second offers a two-week assessment with a report at the end. The third proposes ten hours a week of ongoing leadership. All three proposals sound sensible, and you can't compare them, because each company sells a different job while using much of the same vocabulary.

Sort out which job you need first. The pricing makes sense after that.

## The job each one owns

A **managed service provider**, or MSP, keeps your systems running. Your MSP answers help desk tickets, applies patches, manages devices and backups, and in many contracts watches for security threats. You pay per user or per device each month, and you judge the MSP on uptime and on how fast a ticket gets closed.

A **consultant** answers one defined question and then leaves. You get an assessment, a report or a project plan, with a fixed scope and an end date.

A **fractional CTO** owns your technology direction month after month. That covers the roadmap, vendor selection, AI decisions, and oversight of the MSP and any developers you pay. You judge the fractional CTO on whether the decisions still look right a year later.

## Side by side

| | MSP | Consultant | Fractional CTO |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Owns** | Keeping systems running | One scoped question | Technology direction and decisions |
| **Typical price** | $100 to $250 per user per month, full management ([SecureTechie, May 2026](https://securetechie.com/blog/it-support-cost-small-business/)) | $1,500 to $5,000 per day for audits and workshops ([CTO Input, May 2026](https://blog.ctoinput.com/technology-strategy-consultant-cost/)) | 69.5% of fractional executives charge $5,000 to $10,000 a month ([Fractionus, Oct 2025](https://fractionus.com/blog/fractional-work-statistics-2025-income-market-data)) |
| **Commitment** | Ongoing contract | Ends when the scope ends | Monthly retainer |
| **You measure it on** | Uptime and ticket response | The quality of one recommendation | Decisions that hold up over time |
| **Leaves behind** | Working systems | A report | A roadmap that someone keeps executing |

For a firm of 15 to 30 users, SecureTechie puts all-in managed IT at $2,500 to $5,000 a month. That figure matters when you weigh the other two columns, because you will keep paying it either way.

## The vCIO in the middle

Many MSPs sell a "virtual CIO" add-on, and it looks like a cheaper fractional CTO. One MSP pricing guide describes a common convention of pricing vCIO at around 20 percent of the client's managed services fee ([Scopable, April 2026](https://scopable.io/blog/how-to-price-vcio-services-msp)), and quotes $1,500 to $3,000 a month for firms of 10 to 50 users.

A vCIO can serve a firm well. Know the trade you're making: your strategic adviser works for the company that sells you the services they recommend. If the honest answer to a problem is "change your MSP," a vCIO has a hard time saying it.

## Hiring the wrong one for the job

Watch for two mismatches.

The first is asking your MSP to set strategy. Your MSP earns its fee by keeping tickets moving and systems patched. Roadmaps and vendor negotiations sit outside that contract, and the MSP team gets paid to close tickets, which leaves little room for the hours strategy takes.

The second is hiring a consultant for a problem that keeps coming back. A consultant delivers a sound report on the question you asked in March. By June you have three new questions, and the person who knew your business has moved on to another client.

## Choosing for your firm

Use the problem to pick the help.

**Systems break and repairs take too long.** You need an MSP, or a better one.

**You face one large question with a deadline,** such as a platform migration or a single vendor selection. A consultant or a fixed-scope assessment fits.

**Decisions keep piling up month after month,** and no one on staff can weigh them. That pattern points to a fractional CTO.

Beyond a handful of staff, you'll want an MSP plus one of the other two. A fractional CTO works alongside your MSP and holds it to the standard you're paying for.
