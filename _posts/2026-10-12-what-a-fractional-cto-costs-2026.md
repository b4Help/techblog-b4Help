Many pricing pages in this field ask you to book a call before they show a number. I'd rather put the market data and my rates in one place, with a source next to every figure.

Four sets of numbers matter when you price technology leadership: the full-time salary you'd otherwise pay, what fractional executives charge, what small firms spend on IT today, and what a one-time assessment costs.

## The full-time benchmark

Built In's 2026 salary data puts the average chief technology officer at $224,550. Employers pay $290,000 to $315,000 once they add benefits and payroll costs ([Fractionus, citing Built In](https://fractionus.com/blog/fractional-executive-rates-by-role)).

A full-time hire also brings recruiting time and months of ramp before that person knows your systems. Startups compare fractional fees against that total.

## Fractional retainers, by survey

The Frak Conference State of the Fractional Industry survey reported what fractional executives charge per client each month ([Fractionus](https://fractionus.com/blog/fractional-work-statistics-2025-income-market-data)):

| Monthly retainer | Share of fractional executives |
|---|---|
| Under $5,000 | 29.5% |
| $5,001 to $8,000 | 40.0% |
| $8,001 to $10,000 | 18.5% |
| Over $10,000 | 12.0% |

Seven in ten charge between $5,000 and $10,000. The $15,000 to $25,000 figures quoted on consulting sites describe the top eighth of the market.

Vendux, studying fractional sales leaders in 2024, put their average monthly compensation at $9,651, reported at the same Fractionus link. Sales leadership is a different role, so use that figure as a cross-check on the range.

## The number small firms compare against

A thirty-person professional services firm doesn't measure a fee against a $224,550 salary. The owner measures it against the IT budget.

Securetechie.com puts total IT spend for a firm of 30 to 60 people at $5,000 to $10,000 a month, with fully managed IT running $100 to $250 per user per month ([securetechie.com](https://securetechie.com/blog/it-support-cost-small-business/), May 2026).

Scopable.io reports virtual CIO retainers of $1,500 to $3,000 a month for firms of 10 to 50 users and $3,000 to $8,000 for 50 to 200 users. It also describes an industry habit of pricing vCIO work at about 20% of a client's managed services bill ([scopable.io](https://scopable.io/blog/how-to-price-vcio-services-msp), April 2026). Britecity.com lists CTO advisory retainers at $2,500 to $4,500 a month for firms of 20 to 40 employees ([britecity.com](https://britecity.com/resources/it-consulting/cost-guide/it-consulting-cost-small-business-irvine)).

## One-time assessments

An IT infrastructure assessment for a firm of 10 to 50 employees runs $2,500 to $7,500 ([unio.digital](https://unio.digital/blog/conducting-an-it-infrastructure-assessment-a-checklist)). An assessment gives you a written picture of your systems and a prioritized plan before you commit to a monthly fee.

Retainers convert to effective hourly rates you can compare. A month holds about 43 working hours at ten hours a week, so the $8,500 fractional retainer works out to about $196 an hour, below the $275 ad-hoc rate. The embedded retainer covers about 87 hours a month at about $173 an hour. A retainer also buys availability: you can call when something breaks instead of booking time.

Either assessment fee credits in full against the first month of a retainer that starts within 30 days.

## Reading the numbers for your firm

Start from your firm's size. A firm under 50 people with no compliance load fits the advisory tier, which lands inside the vCIO range above. A firm with a development team, a major migration or regulated data needs the hours in the fractional or embedded tiers.

Then set the fee against two costs: your IT budget, and the price of the last technology decision that went wrong. A five-year contract signed without review can outweigh a year of advice.
