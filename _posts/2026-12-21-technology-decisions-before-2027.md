The last two weeks of December give you something rare: a quiet office and a clear view of next year's calendar. These are the questions I'd want a small firm owner to answer at this point in the year, so you make a few decisions on purpose before the January invoices arrive.

### Should we renew the software contracts that come up in January?

Pull every contract with a renewal date between now and the end of March before you answer that. January renewals slip through because you're busy the week the invoice arrives and you let the automatic payment run.

For each contract, write down the monthly cost, the number of people who log in, and the last time anyone questioned it. Cancel or downgrade anything with a seat count well above its active users. For the rest, a renewal gives you leverage. Ask for a better rate or a shorter term, and be ready to walk away from the ones that won't move.

### Do we need an AI policy if staff use AI for nothing but emails?

Yes, and a short one works fine. Assume some of your staff use AI tools today, approved or not. The policy has to answer two things: which tools staff may use, and which information must stay out of them. Client names, case details, financial records and anything under a confidentiality agreement belong on the second list.

One page covers it. Without that page, each employee makes up their own rules.

### Is our backup good enough?

You can't know until you restore something from it. Last week I wrote about a client who deleted her entire website and got it back within hours, and about the damage a full check found in my own backups. The restore drill in [that post](https://b4help.github.io/techblog-b4Help/2026/12/14/the-day-the-website-disappeared/) takes two hours. Put it on the January calendar now.

### Do we need two-factor authentication on everything?

Turn it on for email, your website host, your domain registrar, your banking and anything that holds client files. Those accounts give an attacker, or a mistaken click, the widest reach. An authenticator app beats text-message codes where the service offers both. Make the change before staff return in January, while inboxes are quiet and fewer people will get locked out mid-task.

### Should AI get its own line in the 2027 budget?

Give it a small line with a specific job attached. A line labelled "AI" with no task attached gives you nothing to measure in June. A line that reads "AI drafting tool for the intake team, measured in hours saved by June" gives you a result to check.

If your firm handles material you can't send to a public cloud, budget for the conversation about private deployment. Models that run on your own hardware cost more to set up and far less in exposure.

### Can our current IT provider handle what we're planning?

Ask them, in writing, and read how they answer. A good managed service provider will tell you which parts of your plan fall inside their contract and which don't. If the answer to every question comes back "yes, we can do that," press for specifics on who does the work and how many hours it will take.

Your IT provider keeps systems running. Setting direction, choosing vendors and deciding where AI fits sit outside that contract.

### Is it worth bringing in outside technology leadership next year?

Count the technology decisions your firm made in 2026 and the ones it postponed. If the postponed list runs longer, you're paying for the gap.

Start with the one-page list: every system, who holds the login, what it costs, and when it renews. Most of the other decisions on this page get easier once that list exists.
